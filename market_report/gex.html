GEX (gamma exposure) estimates how much stock option dealers would have to buy or sell for each 1% move in the underlying, assuming they are long the calls and short the puts that customers hold: Cboe open interest × Black-Scholes gamma (from Cboe implied vol) × 100 × spot² × 1%, calls positive, puts negative. Call wall = strike with the largest positive net GEX; put wall = most negative; gamma flip = strike where cumulative net GEX (from the lowest strike up) bottoms out. Spot above the flip is a positive-gamma regime (dealer hedging tends to dampen moves); below it, negative gamma (hedging tends to amplify them). “Whole book” sums every listed expiry; “Next expiry” uses only the nearest one.

Expected moves are 1σ = at-the-money implied vol × √time, solved from closing quote mids; bands are the close ± 1/2/3σ and ignore skew. Day = to the next session’s close; Week = this week’s last expiry; Month = the standard third-Friday monthly. est = no expiry listed that soon, so the nearest expiry’s IV is scaled by √time (most single stocks on the daily move). ~ = the at-the-money straddle quoted wide (over 20% of mid), so treat the move as approximate. Earnings inside the window are included in the move.

Data: Cboe delayed end-of-day option quotes and open interest, OCC cleared volume for the 500-contract filter (20-session average), computed after each weekday close. The Dow is shown through DIA, its tracking ETF.
